Most AI prototypes impress in a staged demo. Far fewer survive the transition into dependable, production-grade enterprise software that customers and businesses can rely upon day in and day out.

This talk traces the real-world evolution of an AI system from its initial prototype through enterprise deployment, dissecting the architectural and operational decisions that mattered most. We explore the unexpected challenges that only manifest when real users, edge cases, cost constraints, and enterprise security policies collide with probabilistic models.

Focusing on the practical engineering disciplines required to harden AI workflows, the session shares honest architectural retrospectives—what succeeded, what failed, and how teams can bridge the gap between AI experimentation and production dependability.

### Key Takeaways
- **Bridging Demo and Production:** Identify the critical operational differences between an impressive AI prototype and a reliable enterprise service.
- **Hardening Probabilistic Systems:** Implement defensive timeouts, circuit breakers, fallback pipelines, and budget controls around model invocations.
- **Production Edge Cases:** Uncover common failures in real-world traffic, data drift, and unexpected user behavior that sandbox testing misses.
- **Cultural & Organizational Alignment:** Align engineering teams, business stakeholders, and product goals to measure genuine AI value.

### Target Audience
- Engineering managers, technical architects, and developers transitioning AI experiments into production services.

### Prerequisites
- General familiarity with cloud architectures, web APIs, and introductory AI/ML concepts.
